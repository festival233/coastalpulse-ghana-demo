CoastalPulse Ghana — Methods (Prototype v0.1)

How the satellite shoreline-change layers were produced, what they mean, and where they fall short. Nothing is hidden here — the limitations are part of the method.
CoastalPulse Ghana — Prototype v0.1 · Prototype · Not yet field-deployed · Public/open-data analysis · Field validation planned

What this analysis is

An indicative, satellite-derived measurement of shoreline change along the open sandy coast of Keta, Ghana (Keta town frontage + Kedzi coastal strip). It is labeled PUBLIC-OPEN DATA: produced by our analysis of open Copernicus Sentinel-2 imagery — not a field measurement, not survey-grade. It is one input layer of the CoastalPulse Ghana prototype, not a finished product.

Satellite data sources

  • Sensor: Sentinel-2 L2A (10 m resolution), Copernicus / ESA.
  • Discovery: Element84 Earth Search STAC endpoint (earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1), querying the Registry of Open Data on AWS (sentinel-2-l2a).
  • Attribution: contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data (ESA), 2017 & 2024–2025.
  • Early period: 5 scenes, 2016-08-01 → 2017-06-30, cloud cover < 30%.
  • Late period: 6 scenes, 2024-09-01 → 2025-06-30, cloud cover < 30%.
  • Effective dates (composite mid-dates): 2017-03-07 → 2025-01-07 = 7.84 years.

No Google Earth Engine quota was consumed. The analysis ran directly on public STAC + AWS Open Data COGs. No AWS charges were incurred (no requester-pays on this dataset).

Shoreline extraction method

  1. Computed the NDWI water index = (B03 green − B08 NIR) / (B03 + B08) for each scene (McFeeters 1996).
  2. Took the pixel-wise median NDWI across the valid scene stack; water = median NDWI > 0.
  3. Identified the ocean as the largest connected water component (8-connectivity labeling).
  4. Took the shoreline as the 0.5 contour of the ocean mask, converted from UTM 31N to lon/lat.
  5. Pixels with cloud shadow or cloud (SCL classes 3, 8, 9) were excluded; a pixel entered the median only with ≥ 3 valid observations.

Why a median composite (a QA win)

A satellite photo captures the shoreline at whatever tide happened to be out when the satellite passed. A single-date waterline therefore mixes real shoreline change with tidal-stage difference — an artifact of the acquisition, not the coast.

Median compositing averages out tidal-stage variation, so the resulting waterline approximates a mid-tide position rather than an arbitrary acquisition tide.

This was not an abstract concern: our first analysis used single dates (2017-01-04 vs 2025-01-22) and QA found a whole-coast systematic seaward offset in the 2025 scene — tidal-stage bias, not real accretion. That analysis was discarded; its outputs were archived (earth-engine/data/qa_superseded_single_date/) and are not used in the prototype. The median-composite method replaced it.

Transect methodology

  • Transects placed every 100 m along the OpenStreetMap coastline baseline, running west → east.
  • Restricted to the open sandy coast (lon 0.997–1.052): Keta town frontage + Kedzi coastal strip. The Keta inlet, groynes, and lagoon mouth were excluded as artifact-prone.
  • 106 transects. For each, a seaward-normal ray was intersected with each period's waterline; displacement = late − early along the normal; rate = displacement / 7.84 yr.
  • Negative = landward retreat (erosion); positive = seaward advance (accretion).

Known uncertainties

  • ±1 m/yr noise floor. An empirical check on a 5-day scene pair (2025-01-22 → 2025-01-27, n=106) — where no real change could occur — found mean displacement −4.3 m, mean-abs 14.9 m, max-abs 141.1 m. Single-scene waterlines carry ~15 m tidal/method jitter; median compositing reduces it but does not eliminate it. Rates within ±1 m/yr should be treated as noise.
  • ~±2.5 m/yr per-transect method uncertainty (10 m pixels over a 7.84 yr baseline).
  • 10 m pixels → shoreline position uncertain to roughly ±10 m per waterline.

Why our rates differ from published hotspot rates — and why that's expected

Documented rates for this coast are ~8 m/yr background erosion and up to ~17 m/yr immediately down-drift of the Keta Sea Defence (Kedzi side; 2025 Harvard "terminal groyne" study). Our measured transect-median rates (Kedzi −0.66 m/yr) are well below those figures. Verdict: directional/pattern agreement, magnitude shortfall — do not overclaim. Plausible reasons:

  • Documented rates describe specific hotspots/periods; ours is a transect-median over the open sandy coast — different things.
  • The inlet/groyne zone (lon < 0.997, excluded as artifact-prone) may contain the fastest-eroding section.
  • Method uncertainty (~±2.5 m/yr per transect, ±1 m/yr noise floor).
  • Different time windows; and possible real deceleration — cannot be distinguished here.

Limitations — read before using these numbers

  • Residual tidal bias cannot be fully excluded. Median compositing reduces but does not eliminate tidal-stage effects; seasonal beach-profile differences between the two scene windows can leak in.
  • No tidal correction against a tide gauge.
  • No ground-truth GPS survey.
  • Not survey-grade. Not suitable for engineering, compensation, or resettlement decisions.
  • The inlet, groynes, and lagoon mouth are excluded — only the open sandy coast is reported.

Explicit need for field validation: a GPS shoreline survey is required before these rates are used for anything beyond indicative visualization. Field validation is planned, not yet done.

What this prototype does not do

  • Nothing is field-deployed. No sensors, no field measurements by Street Pulse Foundation.
  • The community reporting interface is a prototype with no backend. Demo submissions stay in the browser session only; nothing is transmitted or stored.
  • No AI / machine-learning analysis is built or implied.
